Overview

In 3 months, following the double diamond process with a design team that had no background knowledge of DevOps workflow, I successfully grasped user needs, shipped, and launched part of the system as planned in roadmap 1.

Problem

Tencent has over 10k developers across multiple teams. Different teams used and developed different tools to manage, launch, pack, and deploy code throughout the development process. This situation hindered the corporation not only along the DevOps streamline, but also between the same roles in different teams.

Defining the problem

To solve the problem, Tencent's VP initiated the project and commissioned my team to solve the initial prompt: a cloud-based all-in-one DevOps platform for the company, with a future vision of commercialization.

PMs worked on surveys and communications with managers; we got many insights by working with and learning from them.

What stakeholders care about

Tencent hoped the project to be scalable — growing first to the whole company, then to a product for any company with DevOps needs — and iterative, with 4 milestones and a goal to ship MVP in Jan 2020.

What users care about

I initiated that we empathize with users ourselves. We conducted 6 contextual inquiries with developers and managers across 6 major roles to understand their needs.

Design goal

From the research and quick team alignments, we reframed the problem: Provide a platform that's easy for most DevOps developers and their managers to migrate from current tools and finish their work efficiently.

Design principles

Design and deliver

Feature: Monitoring key metrics

I first reviewed the PRD with PMs and reached agreement on target users, scenarios, tasks, and their flows.

I then analyzed the relationship of components in the interface, generating a layout that best suited the user requirements.

Feature: Adding new items in a form

Across other flows I was responsible for, I found a frequently repeating task: adding new items to a form. After discussing with my team, we decided this interaction was universal enough to add to Tencent's backend design system.

The considerations spanned multiple layers — how it best suits specific use cases, and how it scales to others. I developed 3 alternatives; after several internal critiques, we moved forward with the third.

I then created the "Adding new item to a form" UI component for Tencent's backend design system.

Learning outcomes

Working within while expanding design systems

This project taught me to follow an existing design system while keeping an open mind and making necessary updates to it. That's how an organization maintains consistency from junior to senior designers while functioning in the most efficient ways.

Smart delivery — the best way to hand off to engineers

Delivering design to developers does not necessarily mean a working prototype. There are multiple ways to deliver, depending on existing resources and context.

Scoping, managing, and timing

My design manager showed me how to decide and socialize the scope and methodology the team carries through a project. Designing in an industry setting means shipping within limited resources and time, but with unlimited responsibility and flexibility.
