My role

Research — Investigated the problem space with the market researcher and data analyst to understand opportunities in the checkout experience.

Build and test — Quickly designed and involved stakeholders throughout the process. Tested the final design with real users.

Impact — Presented the design to the leadership team and planned the soft launch of the first steps, impacting 13k current players and 95k potential players.

Team: 1 data analyst, 1 market researcher, 1 PM, 1 marketing specialist, 1 UX researcher

Timeline: Jun 2020 – Aug 2020

Tools: Miro, Figma, Zoom

Takeaways

Move forward with uncertainty — iteration is the nature of product design — In the checkout domain, there are many grey areas and risks that make people too nervous to proceed. There were moments I paused designing to focus purely on research, but I soon realized we can never be confident enough about anything. I changed my approach and started creating concepts, diagrams, and prototypes to show PMs what we'd learned and have developers evaluate dev efforts. "Show instead of tell" is the value designers bring to the team.

Navigate through complicated organizations — Working in a huge organization like EA taught me a mindset: don't block people, and consider the possibility that people might block you. Being intentional about communication paths and approvals is as much a design skill as the craft itself.

Think holistically beyond the screen — The checkout flow design addressed the immediate digital problem, but the full picture — regional payment norms, trust signals, and local UX expectations — mattered just as much as the UI itself.

Take initiative and make an impact — At EA, product designers are expected to think and act like PMs. I took initiative to communicate with the team, served as a point of contact for design-relevant resources, planned work when I identified key missing pieces, and set goals with my PM and teammates.
